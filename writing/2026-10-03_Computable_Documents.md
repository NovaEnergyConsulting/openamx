# Engineers use computable documents

Good engineering requires good documentation.

Good documentation enables clear communication and effective traceability of engineering requirements, calculations, decisions and instructions.

Creating and maintaing good documentation requires a disciplined system of interconnected individual documents. All with different sources, formats and statuses.

These systems have served well the needs of many engineering disciplines for many decades.

However, these systems are also often the source of many engineering issues. Disconnected versions, files that have not been updated, broken references and the list goes on.

We make our best efforts to counteract this entropy through mainly administrative controls. We implement complicated Document Management (DM) systems. We enforce granular permission rules. We designate "champions" in our organisations whose job is essentially to nag everyone to keep their documents up do date.

This represents costs and risks to any organisation managing large amounts of engineering documents.

It doesn't have to be this way. All we need to do is converge into the proverbial "single source of truth". The one document to rule them all.

## The Codification of Engineering Knowledge

In a [previous article](https://lnkd.in/p/gNxk-Vq9), I argued that we do not need to maintain the divide between the **representation** and the **computation** of engineering knowledge. Our mental paradigms are inherited from an era that did not have the tools we have today.

Every engineering task requires two things: an explanation of concepts (the **representation** of knowledge) and calculations that implement those concepts (the **computation**). An electrical engineer will explain how a network is configured and then perform calculations that show the power flows between the various nodes of the network. A mechanical engineer will explain the various options to design a mechanism and then present a table with the calculation of maximum stresses and deflections of each option.

In the past, when **representation** was recorded and **computation** was implemented in physical media, the separation was necessary. Knowledge was represented in clay tablets, papyrus and paper, whilst calculations were performed counting beads, using abacus and ingenious mechanical adding machines.

When knowledge tools became digital, the abstractions we created mimicked the physical media our brains were used to. Knowledge was captured in "documents", calculations performed on "spreadsheets", displayed on "windows" on a "desktop" and stored in "folders".

The need to ease the mental shift into a new digital paradigm, led us down the path of creating digital depictions that matched what we were used to in the physical world. Separate digital files that need to be somehow kept synchronised with the knowledge they represent.

Not only this is not necessary any more, but it is hindering the potential of significant improvements in the way we store knowledge.

This divide should be removed and we should converge into a new paradigm where these two aspects of engineering knowledge, **representation** and **compution** are captured and executed on a single digital object.

## The Holy Grail: A Single Source of Truth

The best way to encode information in a computer is text.
